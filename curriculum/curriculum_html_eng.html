Biologist turned computational scientist, working where biomedical knowledge representation meets deep learning on graphs. My doctoral research asks how much biological semantics is worth formalising in a data representation, and what that formalisation actually buys a learning system: I build ontology-grounded knowledge graphs that carry per-sample multi-omic measurements, and heterogeneous graph neural networks that learn over them for cancer subtype classification and drug–target discovery.

Research lines

  • KG-TransomicNet — a property-graph substrate coupling a PheKnowLator-derived ontological backbone (7.8×105 grounded entities, 1.1×107 typed relations) to the multi-omic profiles of the TCGA/TARGET cohorts at native per-sample precision, keeping entities and measurements jointly queryable in one multimodal database.
  • PathogenKG — heterogeneous knowledge-graph embedding for cross-species antimicrobial drug repurposing: protein interaction networks of 31 bacterial pathogens, orthology, functional annotation and validated drug–target edges in a single graph of over 3 million triples, with a training pipeline designed for extreme class imbalance.
  • MOGNN-TF / MOKG-HGNN — heterogeneous graph neural networks for pan-cancer molecular subtype classification, at the molecular scale with transcription factors as first-class nodes, and at multiple scales through pathway, Gene Ontology and disease nodes.
  • OmicsCouncil — patient representation as the auditable trace of a multi-agent deliberation rather than a dense latent vector, graded against a biomedical knowledge prior.
  • Semantic retrieval and LLM applications — graph-based information retrieval, topic modelling for genomics, retrieval-augmented generation and knowledge-graph-enhanced RAG in the nutrigenetic and transcriptomic domains.
